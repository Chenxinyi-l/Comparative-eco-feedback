Towards a Design Space for Comparative Eco-Feedback

Eco-feedback technology provides feedback on individual or group behaviors with a goal of reducing environmental impact.

[1] Jon Froehlich, Leah Findlater, and James Landay. 2010. The design of eco-feedback technology. In Proceedings of the SIGCHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems (CHI ’10). ACM, New York, NY, USA, 1999–2008. https://doi.org/10.1145/1753326.1753629

Comparative eco-feedback highlights environmental impacts by comparing everyday behaviors with alternatives, reference points, or others’ behaviors. These comparisons help people understand their actions in relation to different values, standards, and collective goals, placing individual behavior in a broader social and collective context.

This website introduces comparative eco-feedback and showcases existing designs through an interactive case library. A heatmap reveals patterns across these cases. A design space and an interactive worksheet help designers explore new possibilities in different contexts.

Context Conditions

Student Housing Energy — Laundry Use

Design Objective
Encourage energy-efficient laundry behaviors among students.
Application Scenario
Shared laundry facilities in student housing, during laundry activities.
Decision Stage
When students plan to do laundry or select washing machine settings.
Temporal Scale
Short, repeated actions over months that gradually form long-term habits.
Product Role
Sustainability is a key goal for housing administration, but may not be a priority for students.
Stakeholders
Students and housing administration.
Available Alternatives
Doing laundry during off-peak hours, choosing energy-saving modes, or washing full loads.
Constraints & User Agency
Laundry room opening hours, booking and collection rules, and noise restrictions. Some changes require support from housing administration.
